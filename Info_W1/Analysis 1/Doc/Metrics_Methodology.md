# Product Metrics and Normalization Methodology

This document explains in simple terms how the four key product metrics were calculated and how they were normalized to a common 1–10 scale for visual comparison.

## 1. How We Computed the Metrics

We evaluated every product across four dimensions using the `Order_ERP.csv` and `Product_master.csv` datasets.

### A. Order Volume
**What it is:** The total number of units sold.
**How it’s computed:** We simply sum up the `Quantity` column for every line item in the orders dataset where the product appears.
**Formula:**
`Order Volume = Σ(Quantities sold across all orders for Product X)`

#### A1. Volume Share %
**What it is:** The percentage of the entire company's sales volume that this product is responsible for over the 3 years.
**Formula:**
`Volume Share % = (Order Volume of Product X / Sum of Order Volume of ALL products) × 100`

### B. Revenue
**What it is:** The total financial value generated by the product.
**How it’s computed:** First, we multiply the `Quantity` of each order line by the product's `Selling Price EUR` to get the line revenue. Then, we sum these line revenues across all orders for the product.
**Formula:**
`Revenue = Σ(Quantity per Order Line × Unit Selling Price)`

#### B1. Revenue Share %
**What it is:** The percentage of the entire company's revenue that this product is responsible for over the 3 years.
**Formula:**
`Revenue Share % = (Revenue of Product X / Sum of Revenue of ALL products) × 100`

### C. Order Rate (Penetration)
**What it is:** The percentage of all unique orders that contain this specific product. It answers the question: "How popular is this product across all baskets?"
**How it’s computed:** 
1. We count the number of *distinct* Order IDs that contain the product.
2. We divide this number by the total number of distinct Order IDs in the entire business.
3. Multiply by 100 to get a percentage.
**Formula:**
`Order Rate = (Number of unique orders containing Product X / Total number of unique orders in business) × 100`

### D. Repeat Rate
**What it is:** The percentage of customers buying this product who purchase it more than once. It measures customer loyalty and recurring demand.
**How it’s computed:** 
1. For a given product, we look at all the unique `Customer ID`s that bought it.
2. We count how many times each customer purchased it.
3. We calculate the percentage of those customers who bought the product 2 or more times.
**Formula:**
`Repeat Rate = (Number of customers who bought Product X multiple times / Total number of customers who bought Product X) × 100`

## 2. How the Normalization Step Works (Scaling from 1 to 10)

The four metrics have entirely different scales. Revenue can be measured in the millions of Euros, Order Volume in thousands of units, while Order Rate and Repeat Rate are percentages from 0% to 100%. 

You cannot plot these directly on a single chart without the largest numbers completely overshadowing the smaller ones. **To solve this, we normalize all four metrics to a universal 1 to 10 scale.**

### The Method: Percentile Ranking

Instead of standard mathematical scaling (which gets easily distorted if one product sells 100 times more than all the others), we used **Percentile Rank Scaling**. 

Here is how it works:
1. **Rank the products:** For each metric, all products are ordered from worst to best.
2. **Assign a Percentile:** A product's score is based on what percentage of the portfolio it beats.
   * If a product is better than 90% of the other products in Revenue, it gets a 90% rank (0.90).
   * If a product is in the exact middle, it gets a 50% rank (0.50).
3. **Convert to 1-10 Scale:** We apply the formula.

**Final Scaling Formula:**
`Scaled Score (1 to 10) = 1 + (Percentile Rank × 9)`

### Why this method is the best:
* **Removes Outlier Distortion:** If your top product sells 1,000,000 units and the second-best sells 5,000 units, standard scaling would score the second-best product near 1. Percentile scaling simply says "Product 1 is the 100th percentile, Product 2 is the 99th percentile", scoring them as 10 and 9.9 respectively.
* **Forces Distribution:** It guarantees that you have a smooth, even distribution of scores completely across the 1-10 scale, making radar charts and quadrant plots beautifully intuitive and easy to read.

### Example:
If a product has an **Order Rate Percentile Rank of 0.82** (meaning its order rate is higher than 82% of all other products):
`Score = 1 + (0.82 * 9) = 1 + 7.38 = 8.38` (approximated to 8.4 on the 1-10 scale).
