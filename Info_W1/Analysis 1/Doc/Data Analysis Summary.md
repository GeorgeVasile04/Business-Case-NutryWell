# Data Analysis Summary: Order-to-Delivery Diagnostics
**Context:** This document outlines the step-by-step diagnostic analysis performed on the NutrYWell Week 1 operational data. The goal was to mathematically determine whether the current operational tensions are due to simple execution problems or deeper structural flaws stemming from the recent acquisition.

---

## 1. Order Entry & Information Flow Analysis
**What we analyzed:** The time it takes for a customer's order to become visible to the warehouse workers. 
**How it was done:** We measured the lag between the moment the customer placed the order and the moment it was registered in the backend ERP system.
**Mathematical Computation:** `Entry Lag Days = ERP Entry Date - Order Date`
**Results:**
* Total unique orders evaluated: 11,946 (containing 19,981 product lines)
* Average Lag: 1.62 days
* Maximum Lag: 15 days
* Percentage of orders delayed (>0 days lag): 56.69%

**Conclusion:** Over half of all orders suffer an administrative delay before the warehouse even knows they exist. This validates the hypothesis that the temporarily separated IT systems (caused by the Transitional Service Agreement) are structurally eating into the preparation window, forcing operational teams into constant "rush" modes.

---

## 2. Order Splitting & Digital Silos
**What we analyzed:** Whether the system is artificially fragmenting customer purchases into multiple redundant orders due to poor IT integration across the two product portfolios (Core vs. Lifestyle).
**Mathematical Computation:** We grouped the data by `Customer ID` and `Order Date` to represent a single "Shopping Basket". We then counted how many different physical warehouses (`Branch/DC`) were invoked to fulfill that single basket.
**Results:**
* Total unique Customer-Day Baskets: 8,440
* Total Baskets split across multiple DCs: 3,426
* Split Percentage: 40.59%

**Conclusion:** Although the ERP recorded 11,946 orders, customers really only placed 8,440 "baskets". In 40.59% of transactions, the IT system forcefully split the single basket into multiple distinct Order IDs simply because the products resided in different warehouses (BE-DC1 vs BE-DC2). This destroys any attempt at outbound consolidation.

---

## 3. Delivery Reliability & Service Level (OTIF)
**What we analyzed:** On-Time Delivery performance to see where NutrYWell is struggling the most.
**How it was done:** We calculated the variance between the day the goods physically arrived and the day they were expected.
**Mathematical Computation:** `Actual Receipt Date - Customer Requested Date` (If <= 0, it is considered On-Time).
**Results:**
* Global On-Time vs Requested Date: 79.40%
* By Channel Breakdown:
  * Pharmacy: 81.98%
  * Retail: 90.30%
  * Retail Sport: 86.42%
  * **E-commerce: 49.20%**

**Conclusion:** The legacy distribution network performs reasonably well for traditional retail, but it fundamentally fails to handle the fast-paced, direct-to-consumer logic required by the newly acquired E-commerce segment. This is a structural network mismatch.

---

## 4. Logistics Inefficiencies and Network Fragmentation
**What we analyzed:** The financial and physical waste occurring during outbound transport.
**How it was done & Assumptions:** Logistics carriers charge using a "Less-Than-Truckload" (LTL) tariff model, which dictates a base fixed cost per pallet. 
*   *Assumption:* The carrier treats every unique `Order ID` generated by a specific DC as an isolated shipping unit and applies the minimum 1-pallet charge accordingly. This is a robust assumption because it reflects standard LTL billing practices—carriers do not cross-reference disparate picking tickets unless legally manifested to do so.
**Mathematical Computation:** 
1. Grouped all lines by `Order ID` and `DC` to define "Physical Shipments" (Truck Stops/Bills). 
2. Calculated Weight: `Total Weight = Sum(Quantity * Net Weight kg)`.
3. Calculated Pallets Required: `Ceiling(Total Weight / Min Pallet Capacity of 200kg)`.
4. Calculated Fill Rate: `Average Weight per Pallet / 200kg`.
**Results:**
* Total Physical Shipments (invoiced): 11,946  
* Total physical pallets paid for: 12,275
* Average Weight per Pallet: 28.7 kg (Max capacity: 200kg)
* Overall Pallet Fill Rate: 14.4%
* Shipments weighing < 50 kg: 9,980 (83.5% of total)

**Conclusion:** The most catastrophic finding is that 83.5% of the physical shipments leaving the DCs weigh less than 50kg. Because the separated networks force massive order fragmentation, NutrYWell is paying the strict base pallet rate for pallets that are 85% empty (14.4% fill rate). The company is spending millions annually to transport plastic wrap and empty air.

---

### **Executive Summary**
The analyzed constraints are **not** isolated operational challenges; they are deep, systemic structural flaws caused by the dual IT and dual physical logic of the transition period. 
1. Separated IT creates 1.6 days of blind administrative lag.
2. 40%+ of customer orders are violently split between warehouses.
3. E-commerce promises are failing 50% of the time.
4. Logistics costs are drastically inflated by paying for 14.4% filled pallets.

**Recommendation:** Incremental process tweaks at the warehouse floor will not resolve these issues. A fundamental redesign of the Order-to-Delivery operating model (specifically IT integration and physical network consolidation) must be executed before the Transitional Service Agreement expires.
