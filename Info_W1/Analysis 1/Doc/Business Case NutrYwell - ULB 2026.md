**BUSINESS CASE**

**NutrYWell Laboratories**

**From Order-to-Delivery Pressure to Strategic Supply Chain Design**

**1. Company Background**

NutrYWell Laboratories is a European company operating in the human nutritional supplements industry. The company develops, markets, and distributes vitamins, probiotics, immunity boosters, and performance-oriented nutritional products.

Over the past five years, NutrYWell has experienced strong commercial growth, driven by:

- Expansion in pharmacy retail networks
- Rapid development of its e-commerce channel
- Increasing consumer demand for preventive health products

To strengthen its value proposition and accelerate portfolio expansion, NutrYWell recently acquired a non-core activity from a large international group primarily specialized in sports accessories and equipment.

This parent group decided to refocus on its core business (sports apparel and accessories) and divest its nutritional supplements activity.

For NutrYWell, this acquisition represented a strategic opportunity to:

- Complement its existing portfolio with performance-oriented products
- Access a new customer base
- Accelerate its presence in the sports nutrition segment

The acquisition includes:

- The full product portfolio of the divested activity
- The production capabilities associated with these products
- The entire client portfolio linked to this activity

However, prior to the acquisition, nutritional products were distributed using the parent group’s established logistics and distribution network.


**2. Transitional Phase**

Following the acquisition, NutrYWell entered into a transitional service agreement (TSA) with the divesting group.

During this transition period:

- NutrYWell temporarily continues to use the former parent company’s distribution network
- Warehousing and transportation flows remain partially operated through the legacy network
- Information systems and order processing interfaces remain partly separated

This transitional setup was designed to ensure business continuity and avoid disruption to customers.

However, this temporary configuration creates several structural challenges:

- Dual distribution logic
- Limited end-to-end visibility
- Complex allocation rules
- Dependence on external logistics processes is not fully controlled by NutrYWell

The transition period is contractually limited and will end within a defined timeframe, requiring NutrYWell to progressively internalize or redesign its distribution model.


**3. Current Situation**

Despite sustained market demand and strong commercial momentum, NutrYWell is experiencing increasing pressure across its operations.

Over the past twelve months, performance reviews have revealed a widening gap between growth ambitions and execution outcomes. While overall activity levels remain high, delivery reliability and planning stability appear more difficult to maintain in a consistent and predictable manner.

Several indicators suggest emerging tensions within the Order-to-Delivery process, including fluctuations in service performance, variability in product availability, and a growing need for short-term operational adjustments.

At the same time, operational teams at warehouse and transport level continue to demonstrate strong execution discipline and responsiveness. Day-to-day issues are frequently resolved through close coordination and significant additional effort.

However, this reliance on reactive adjustments and operational intensity raises a fundamental question regarding the long-term robustness and scalability of the current operating model.




**4. Structural Complexity After the Acquisition**

The recent acquisition has significantly increased the structural complexity of NutrYWell’s operations.

While business continuity has been maintained, early observations suggest that the company is now operating in a more diversified and demanding environment than before.

This evolving context requires a careful assessment of:

- The nature and sources of operational complexity
- The degree of heterogeneity within the portfolio and customer base
- The impact of integration choices on planning and execution
- The sustainability of the current operating model

Students are expected to identify, characterize, and measure the different dimensions of complexity affecting the Order-to-Delivery process, and assess whether the existing supply chain model is adequately adapted to this new context.


**5. Observed Symptoms**

Recent performance reviews and cross-functional discussions suggest recurring tensions within the Order-to-Delivery process.

While individual functions remain highly committed and operationally engaged, several patterns indicate that current performance levels may not be fully sustainable in a context of continued growth.

Preliminary observations point to:

- Increasing operational pressure across functions
- Frequent adjustments and escalations to secure delivery commitments
- Growing interdependencies between commercial decisions and operational constraints
- Limited visibility beyond the short-term planning horizon
- A need to reassess the effectiveness of existing coordination mechanisms

Execution teams are often required to absorb variability and resolve issues in real time, raising questions about the robustness of the current operating model.

This situation invites deeper reflection:

Are the observed performance gaps primarily the result of isolated operational challenges, or do they reflect broader structural dynamics within NutrYWell’s management system?


**6. The Challenge**

NutrYWell’s Executive Committee has mandated a cross-functional task force to conduct a structured diagnostic of the order-to-delivery process. With the end of the transitional agreement approaching, the company must determine whether incremental adjustments will be sufficient, or whether a more fundamental redesign of its operating model is required.
